This was my first expo and first booth. RITHM ran it side by side with PROFEX Academy in a dual-booth setup. That pairing was the point of the weekend, not a coincidence.

## The event

Biohackers World’s first New York Conference & Expo, and its East Coast debut. Organizers reported more than 1,500 attendees, 30+ speakers, and 60+ exhibiting companies over two days at the Barclay. The public recap on July 2, 2026 lists RITHM™ among participating exhibitors. The event page listed RITHM™ as a gold exhibitor.

Title sponsors were Leela Quantum Tech and N1O1 by Pneuma Nitric Oxide. The floor mixed diagnostics, wearables, recovery, supplements, and longevity tools. Coverage of the New York debut framed the room the same way the post did: people already had numbers. They wanted to know what to do with them.

## What the booth actually was

PROFEX ran the live diagnostics. RITHM existed for what happens after those results leave the booth.

| Partner | Role at the booth |
|---|---|
| **PROFEX Academy** | Clinical-grade diagnostics: InBody body composition analysis, paired with FOOTWORK PRO plantography. Data on what is happening inside the body, and how it shows up in stance and gait. |
| **RITHM** | The product after the snapshot. Built on three things: **biometrics, behavior, and context**. PROFEX supplied the biometrics. Behavior is what someone actually does day to day. Context is sleep, stress, environment, and the rest of their life. |

A diagnostic without behavior and context is a snapshot. RITHM is what turns that snapshot into something tracked and acted on over time. That is the same gap BariAccess is built to close.

That split matches how RITHM is described on the Biohackers World exhibitor page: not another standalone tracker, but a health and performance layer built around behavior, biometrics, and timing, incorporating in-person assessment through The PROFEX Method, and meant to sit inside practices, studios, gyms, and coaching environments.

PROFEX’s published diagnostics match the booth tools:

- Body-structure analysis on Biospace InBody analyzers (Body 770 / Body 720): fat and muscle distribution, water, proteins, minerals, basal metabolism, visceral fat, and left/right side of the body.
- Foot function on the AmCube FootWork Pro / GaitScan platform: 400+ pressure sensors, up to 300 readings per second, used to evaluate stance, gait, and left/right imbalance.

Prof. Duško Ilić ran the one-on-one result walkthroughs. PROFEX describes those exams as his author method: quantified results are not enough without a detailed body check.

## What I did on the floor

The opening of the post is the first hour: an attendee looked at her own body scan, asked why one side was carrying more than the other, and left knowing why and what to change. Footwear was working against her. The recommendation was specific, not a generic “optimize.” Prof. Ilić walked her through the results. That conversation is the booth in one scene.

My work was the live operation around those conversations:

- First-time booth and first-time expo, running it with the RITHM team on the ground.
- Dual-booth setup with PROFEX, not a standalone RITHM table.
- Two guided quizzes, built to teach rather than only assess: one on PROFEX diagnostics, one on RITHM essentials.
- Prep that made two days of one-on-one diagnostics possible: staff flow documentation, quiz logic, and intake forms.

The operational claim to keep: good operations mattered as much as good technology. Prep and flow are what kept live diagnostics from turning into a queue.

## What stood out (from the post)

- The health-optimization space is moving past supplements and wearables. The serious conversations were about clinical-grade diagnostics, behavioral health tech, and patient-facing tools.
- Almost everyone who stopped wanted more than a number. They wanted the next action. That is the RITHM / BariAccess gap.
- Running a booth taught as much about live problem solving as months of building.
