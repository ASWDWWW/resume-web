## What this role usually means

Infrastructure engineers own compute, network, capacity, IAM landing zones, and reliability of fleets. Terraform, cloud accounts, and incident response at infra layer.

## What you actually did on FitGenius

FitGenius is a pre-launch consumer fitness product: iOS and Android (Expo / React Native), Firebase backend (~64 Cloud Functions, Firestore rules, IAP verification, Gemini AI), marketing site (Vite/React), app/legal/admin hosting, store-submission runbooks, and a legal/compliance suite. You were the founding technical partner — one person owning product, code, cloud, QA packets, and founder enablement from roughly May through September 2026.

You configured Firebase Hosting, Functions region, Secret Manager, Firestore indexes, emulators, and GitHub Actions runners. That is application infrastructure. You did not design VPCs, load balancers, or capacity plans.

## How that work applies to **Infrastructure Engineer**

Thin overlap. Useful only for small-company 'infra' titles that really mean 'you deploy the app.' Do not apply to core infra/SRE fleet jobs.

## Strengths a hiring manager can credit

- Managed-cloud setup that actually runs
- Backup/restore notes and environment doc
- Security logging query documented

## Gaps versus a typical hiring bar

- No Terraform/Pulumi
- No staging account strategy
- No network or OS-level infra

## How to talk about it

Prefer Cloud Engineer or Backend. If a startup says infrastructure and means Firebase, you fit.
