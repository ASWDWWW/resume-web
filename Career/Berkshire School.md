# Berkshire School

Four-year boarding student at [Berkshire School](https://www.berkshireschool.org/) in Sheffield, Massachusetts (Class of 2020). Academic focus was engineering, mathematics, and science. Senior-year prefect. Played football all four years.

Berkshire sits on a 400-acre campus at the base of Mount Everett in the Berkshires. It is a co-ed New England college-prep boarding and day school, founded in 1907. About 430 students, 4:1 student-to-faculty ratio, average class size of 12, and most students live on campus. The motto is *Pro Vita Non Pro Schola Discimus* — learning, not just for school, but for life.

I came from Elizabeth, New Jersey. Living in a dorm under the Mountain for four years was the first time school, sports, and daily life were the same place. That is where the engineering path started in a serious way, before St. Lawrence University’s 3+2 combined plan and Columbia Engineering.

## Boarding for four years (2016–2020)

Berkshire’s dorms use vertical housing: students from every grade share a hall, with faculty dorm parents in adjoining apartments. Study hours run in the evening (typically 8:00–10:00 p.m.). That structure is the boarding-school part people skip over. Homework, labs, and football practice were not separate from how the day was built.

What four years as a boarder actually meant:

- Learning to run a week without going home: classes, study hall, meals, practice, and weekend campus life.
- Living with students from other states and countries, not only New Jersey.
- Upperclassmen as the people younger students actually watch, not just faculty.
- The habit of finishing work in a quiet dorm, then showing up for the next thing.

By Form VI I was one of the seniors those younger boarders were watching. That is why prefect was the job that fit.

## Academic focus: engineering, mathematics, and science

The academic through-line was STEM. Berkshire’s curriculum and signature programs are built for that: a large course list (about 129 courses, including 21 APs), plus offerings that go past a standard high-school science sequence — Advanced Math/Science Research, Introduction to Engineering (microcontrollers, circuitry, 3D printing, iterative design), computer science, calculus through multivariable, physics, chemistry, biology, astronomy at the Dixon Observatory, and Advanced Environmental Science Research.

I used that environment to load mathematics and science and to treat engineering as the point of the work, not an elective on the side. Small classes and a 4:1 faculty ratio made it possible to stay in hard STEM courses instead of backing off. The same focus is what the 3+2 path required later: calculus through differential equations, university physics, chemistry, and programming at St. Lawrence, then Computer Engineering at Columbia.

This is the high-school version of that path. Problem sets, labs, and quantitative classes were the main academic work for four years, with engineering as the direction those subjects were aimed at.

## Sophomore year: Pro Vita dogsledding in Minnesota

Each winter, Berkshire pauses the regular schedule for [Pro Vita Winter Session](https://www.berkshireschool.org/academics/signature-programs/pro-vita-winter-session) — a week of courses, speakers, and off-campus trips built around the school motto. Students pick from more than 50 options. Faculty-chaperoned trips have included dogsledding in Minnesota, along with other travel programs.

Sophomore year (Form IV) I went to Minnesota on the Pro Vita dogsledding trip. It was not a classroom week. Cold, dogs, trail, and a group of Berkshire students far from Sheffield. The point of Pro Vita is to take a subject seriously outside the trimester calendar. That trip is the one that stuck: you do not fake competence in that weather, and you learn how a team actually moves when the work is physical and the conditions are the problem.

## Senior year: prefect

Prefects are selected in the spring of Form V to serve as student leaders in Form VI. They mentor younger students, help run daily dorm life (study hall, lights out, dorm meetings), stay visible on campus, and sit between students and faculty. Each dorm typically has two senior prefects alongside live-in dorm parents. Berkshire describes the role as informal guidance plus a consistent presence — not a title you wear only at all-school events.

I was a prefect my senior year. That meant:

- Being the older student younger boarders could actually find after study hall.
- Helping the dorm run: study hours, lights out, meetings, weekend activities.
- Setting a standard in the same hall I had lived in since Form III.
- Doing that job while still carrying a STEM course load and football.

Prefect is residential leadership. It is closer to running a small community than to a club officer line on a resume. It is also why the boarding years were not only academics: by senior year the work was keeping the dorm honest while finishing the academic path that led to college engineering.

## Football (all four years)

I played football at Berkshire all four years (2020 class): middle linebacker, center, and running back. Practice and games were the afternoon half of the same boarding day as math and science. A longer record of that, and of college football after Berkshire, is in `Football Career.md`.

## What it led to

Berkshire was the four-year boarding foundation for a mathematics and engineering college path:

| Years | Place | Role |
|---|---|---|
| 2016–2020 | Berkshire School, Sheffield, MA | Boarding student; STEM focus; prefect (Form VI); football |
| 2020–2023 | St. Lawrence University | B.A. path in Mathematics; 3+2 pre-engineering core |
| 2022 | James Cook University (Townsville) | Study-abroad semester inside the combined plan |
| 2023–2025 | Columbia Engineering | B.S., Computer Engineering (Combined Plan) |

The school’s own language is the right summary of what those years were for: an inclusive community where students are known well, a demanding academic program, and preparation for a life of purpose — in this case, engineering.
