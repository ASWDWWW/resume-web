# Weave News

- Contributed to [Weave News](https://www.weavenews.org/authors/zakiy-manigo) as a writer while studying abroad at James Cook University (College of Science and Engineering) in Townsville, Queensland, Australia, June–November 2022.

- Published “Sharks and Rays Belong on Australia’s Protected Species Lists” on April 7, 2023 as Analysis / News in The Glocal Exchange series: [https://www.weavenews.org/stories/2023/4/7/sharks-and-rays-belong-on-australias-protected-species-lists](https://www.weavenews.org/stories/2023/4/7/sharks-and-rays-belong-on-australias-protected-species-lists)

- Wrote the piece after living on the Great Barrier Reef coast. Townsville, Magnetic Island, Castle Hill, and The Strand made marine conservation a local issue, not just a class topic. Coursework in data mining, probability, game design, human rights, and social justice shaped how I researched and argued the story.

- The article covers Australia’s Threatened Species Action Plan (October 2022), the first time marine life was included. Of 100 new species, only about a quarter were marine and only nine were fish, even though more than 45,000 marine animals in Australia face endangerment.

- Main argument: sharks and rays are still treated as “out of sight, out of mind.” Australia has more than a quarter of the world’s sharks and rays, and 62 species in Australian waters are Endangered, Threatened, or Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List, but only 15 are protected under Australian law. Species left off the plan include hammerheads, great whites, and bow-mouth guitarfish.

- Also reported that conservation attention still goes to terrestrial mammals (“mammal extinction capital of the world”), that La Niña and marine heatwaves add pressure the plan does not name, and that losing top predators can trigger a trophic cascade. Shark and ray populations have declined by over 70% globally since 1970.

- Author page bio at the time: first-generation student from Elizabeth, New Jersey; dual bachelor’s in Mathematics and Computer Engineering through St. Lawrence University’s 3+2 Combined Engineering program.
