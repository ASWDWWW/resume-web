## What the 3+2 plan is

St. Lawrence does not offer a standalone undergraduate engineering major. Instead it runs a dual-degree **combined plan** with four engineering schools: [Clarkson, Columbia, Dartmouth (Thayer), and Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute](https://www.stlawu.edu/offices/engineering-combined-programs/participating-universities).

The usual sequence is:

1. **Three years at St. Lawrence** — a liberal-arts bachelor’s major plus a fixed pre-engineering core (calculus through differential equations, university physics, chemistry, and introductory programming), plus St. Lawrence distribution and diversity requirements.
2. **Two years at the partner engineering school** — the junior and senior years of an engineering B.S.
3. **Two bachelor’s degrees** — one from each institution — awarded when the engineering degree is finished.

St. Lawrence’s [pre-professional / dual-degree overview](https://www.stlawu.edu/academics/pre-professional-and-dual-degree-programs) states the same definition: three years of foundational coursework at St. Lawrence, then two years of specialized study at the partner, with degrees from both schools at the end.

That is why a St. Lawrence record for a 3+2 student looks like **three academic years**, not four, and why the Columbia record **starts at the engineering junior year** with a large block of transfer credit.

Program rules that matter when reading the St. Lawrence transcript ([program description](https://www.stlawu.edu/offices/engineering-combined-programs/program-description)):

- All St. Lawrence general-education, graduation, and 3+2 major requirements must be met **before** enrolling at the engineering school.
- At least **25 semester units** must be earned at St. Lawrence; grades below **2.0** do not count toward that minimum.
- Students declare a St. Lawrence major in the second year and complete **three years of that major’s 3+2 option**.
- The St. Lawrence degree is issued **after** the engineering degree is completed.

On St. Lawrence’s scale, **1.0 unit ≈ 3.6 semester hours**, and a normal full-time load is four 1.0-unit courses. Columbia’s evaluation of that work appears on the Columbia transcript as **68.00 credits transferred from St. Lawrence University**.

## Years 4–5 at Columbia (the “+2”)

Columbia’s Combined Plan is normally a **3-2**: three years of liberal arts, apply in the junior year, then two years at [Columbia Engineering](https://undergrad.admissions.columbia.edu/apply/combinedplan). Affiliate-college applicants are prioritized. Combined-plan Computer Engineering students are expected to arrive having finished the equivalent of Columbia’s first two years, including programming / data structures, discrete math, and introductory electrical engineering background, then to complete the junior–senior Computer Engineering curriculum in two years.

The Columbia transcript is that junior–senior sequence. Fall 2023 is the standard incoming 3-2 Computer Engineering load:

- `COMS W3134` Data Structures in Java
- `ELEN E3201` / `E3081` Circuit Analysis + lab
- `ELEN E3801` / `E3084` Signals and Systems + lab
- `IEOR E3658` Probability for Engineers

Later terms continue the Computer Engineering major (advanced programming, theory, computer systems, digital / electronic circuits and labs, programming languages, UI, AI, networks, architecture, databases, embedded systems, and related electives) through **Spring 2025**. The degree line is **Bachelor of Science, 21 May 2025**.

The **68.00 credits transferred from St. Lawrence** are Columbia’s official conversion of the liberal-arts / pre-engineering years (including the evaluated study-abroad work). Those credits are why the Columbia record does not repeat Calculus I–III, physics, chemistry, and introductory programming as Columbia course numbers.
